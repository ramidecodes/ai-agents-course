# AI Agents for Your Business

*Build a persistent AI workspace for strategy, brand, content, and execution*

> Draft status: the course direction and lesson sequence are defined. The demonstrated memory solution, exact setup steps, downloadable skills, examples, and timings must be validated before recording.

## Course promise

Learners will turn scattered business knowledge and isolated AI conversations into a persistent, practical workspace. They will create an inspectable memory system, organize approved business context, add reusable skills, and run recurring workflows for strategy, brand, content, and execution.

The goal is not an AI system that remembers everything or runs the business unattended. It is a controlled workspace that retrieves the right context, preserves confirmed knowledge, supports useful work, and remains understandable to its owner.

## Course description

This course builds on **AI Agents Fundamentals**. Learners move beyond isolated tasks and set up an AI workspace that can use approved context across sessions. They learn the practical differences among instructions, source knowledge, working context, memory, retrieval, and history; then apply that architecture to business planning, brand, content, projects, decisions, and recurring reviews.

Theory is paired with guided setup. All examples, templates, skills, and assignments are designed for entrepreneurs, creators, coaches, consultants, and small teams. The learner remains responsible for strategic choices, factual claims, permissions, sensitive information, and consequential actions.

## Intended audience

This course is for:

- Entrepreneurs and small-business owners building a persistent AI workspace.
- Creators, consultants, and coaches who repeatedly re-explain their context to AI tools.
- Personal-brand builders who need consistent strategy, voice, and content workflows.
- Small teams that want a clear path from private context to shared project knowledge.
- Learners ready to create or install reusable skills and connect additional capabilities.

It is not a course on agent engineering, custom model training, production multi-agent infrastructure, or fully autonomous business operations.

## Prerequisites

Learners need:

- The skills taught in **AI Agents Fundamentals** or equivalent experience.
- A supported AI assistant and permission to configure its workspace or connections.
- One real business, brand, project, or creator practice to use as a case.
- A small set of non-sensitive materials such as a website, offer description, customer notes, plans, or published content.
- Permission to use and store the selected materials.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, learners will be able to:

1. Set up and verify a persistent AI workspace with inspectable canonical sources and a replaceable retrieval or memory layer.
2. Design a useful business ontology and manage the lifecycle of private, project, and shared knowledge.
3. Create an approved business, audience, offer, brand, and content source of truth grounded in evidence.
4. Build reusable skills and supervised workflows for planning, research, decisions, content, and recurring reviews.
5. Complete one recurring business workflow and maintain its context through capture, confirmation, retrieval, correction, and archiving.

## Completion criteria

A learner completes the course when they can:

- Explain where instructions, source knowledge, working context, memory, retrieval, and session history belong.
- Run a setup acceptance test that writes, retrieves, corrects, and removes disposable context.
- Distinguish a confirmed fact or decision from evidence, a hypothesis, and an agent inference.
- Assign scope, source, owner, status, sensitivity, and review timing to important knowledge.
- Build an approved business context pack without silently inventing missing information.
- Create a reusable skill with explicit inputs, process, output, quality criteria, and approval points.
- Complete and review one recurring workflow using the persistent workspace.
- Correct or supersede stale knowledge and verify that the current version is retrieved.

# Detailed curriculum draft

## Module 1 — Build the persistent workspace

**Module purpose:** Move from an off-the-shelf assistant used for isolated tasks to a workspace that can preserve and retrieve approved context across sessions.

### Lesson 1 — Design the workspace before choosing the memory product

**Objective:** Identify the layers of a durable, replaceable agent workspace.  
**Learner output:** A workspace architecture showing the assistant, canonical sources, active context, retrieval layer, tools, skills, and approval boundaries.

Key points:

- Separate the agent interface from the knowledge the learner owns.
- Keep important context in inspectable canonical sources.
- Treat retrieval indexes, summaries, and graphs as replaceable views.
- Decide which information is private, project-specific, shared, or temporary.
- Choose one primary demonstrated path and retain a plain-file fallback.

### Lesson 2 — Set up and verify persistent context

**Objective:** Install or configure the selected memory solution and prove that its basic lifecycle works.  
**Learner output:** A workspace that passes the setup acceptance test.

Acceptance test:

1. Identify the active workspace and its permissions.
2. Read one harmless starter source.
3. Write a clearly labeled disposable item.
4. Inspect the stored result outside the conversation.
5. Retrieve it in a fresh session.
6. Correct it and verify the correction.
7. Remove it and confirm that it no longer influences retrieval.
8. Export or back up the canonical sources.

Closing rule: **do not trust persistent context until you can inspect, correct, retrieve, export, and delete it.**

## Module 2 — Design memory that remains trustworthy

**Module purpose:** Replace the idea of magical memory with an explicit information architecture and maintenance lifecycle.

### Lesson 3 — Instructions, context, knowledge, history, retrieval, and memory

**Objective:** Decide where each kind of information belongs and how it reaches the model.  
**Learner output:** An information-classification map for the learner’s workspace.

Key points:

- Instructions define reusable behavior; sources contain material the agent may consult.
- Working context is temporary; history is a record; memory is selected state made available again.
- RAG retrieves material into context but does not prove its authority or truth.
- Embeddings help find semantic similarity; graphs make relationships explicit.
- Native product memory is convenient personalization, not necessarily the canonical business record.

### Lesson 4 — Build the ontology and memory lifecycle

**Objective:** Define the smallest useful business ontology and govern how knowledge changes.  
**Learner output:** A starter ontology plus one confirmed, sourced memory.

Starter entities:

- Profile, business, audience, offer, brand, and content system.
- Goal, project, task, decision, experiment, evidence, person, and session.

Important metadata:

- Scope, source, status, effective date, confidence, owner, sensitivity, and review date.

Lifecycle:

> Search → propose → confirm → write → retrieve → correct or supersede → archive or delete.

## Module 3 — Give the agent your business context

**Module purpose:** Turn scattered business material into a compact, approved source of truth.

### Lesson 5 — Capture the business operating context

**Objective:** Create a concise view of the business, current goals, constraints, projects, decisions, and evidence.  
**Learner output:** An approved business profile and active-work brief.

Key points:

- Record mission, business model, stage, constraints, goals, owners, and current projects.
- Separate facts, decisions, hypotheses, evidence, and generated suggestions.
- Preserve rationale and supersession for important decisions.
- Leave unknown fields unresolved rather than inviting the agent to fill them plausibly.

### Lesson 6 — Define audience, offer, positioning, and brand

**Objective:** Build an AI-ready brand foundation grounded in approved sources and audience evidence.  
**Learner output:** Audience, offer, positioning, and brand notes with provenance.

Key points:

- Capture audience tensions, desired outcomes, objections, and supporting evidence.
- Define offer scope, exclusions, proof, approved claims, and unresolved questions.
- Record positioning, promise, differentiators, values, and brand boundaries.
- Treat strategic synthesis as a proposed decision until the learner approves it.

## Module 4 — Build the brand and content system

**Module purpose:** Translate approved business context into repeatable editorial decisions and production methods.

### Lesson 7 — Define voice, content pillars, channels, and formats

**Objective:** Make a small set of editorial choices that connect audience needs to business goals.  
**Learner output:** A voice card, approved example library, content pillars, and channel-role map.

Key points:

- Define voice through principles, ranges, patterns, anti-patterns, and examples.
- Give every pillar a purpose, evidence base, point of view, and boundary.
- Give every channel and format a job beyond “reach.”
- Mark untested editorial choices as hypotheses with review dates.

### Lesson 8 — Create the supervised content-production system

**Objective:** Build reusable methods for research, ideation, briefing, drafting, editing, repurposing, and review.  
**Learner output:** A content workflow and review rubric.

Key points:

- Ground research and claims in labeled sources.
- Use a brief containing audience, tension, claim, evidence, action, constraints, format, and standard.
- Separate structural, factual, voice, and final-quality reviews.
- Repurpose the approved idea for the channel instead of copying the artifact.
- Require human approval before external publication.

## Module 5 — Create skills for recurring business work

**Module purpose:** Turn successful methods into portable playbooks that can work with different assistants and tools.

### Lesson 9 — Build reusable skills and connect capabilities

**Objective:** Create a skill that clearly separates method, context, tools, permissions, and approval.  
**Learner output:** One tested skill for a recurring business or content task.

A useful skill defines:

- Trigger and intended outcome.
- Required context and authoritative sources.
- Normal process and decision points.
- Available tools or MCP connections.
- Deliverable and quality checklist.
- Stop-and-ask rules, recovery, and memory writes.

### Lesson 10 — Design entrepreneur workflows and coaching routines

**Objective:** Choose recurring work that benefits from persistent context without handing over business accountability.  
**Learner output:** A workflow map for planning, review, research, decisions, or content.

Candidate workflows:

- Session start and close.
- Weekly business review.
- Goal and project review.
- Decision preparation and decision log.
- Customer-research synthesis.
- Content research and production.
- Reflection, challenge, planning, and accountability prompts.

The agent may help the learner reflect and prepare decisions, but it should not present itself as a replacement for qualified professional advice.

## Module 6 — Run, review, and maintain the system

**Module purpose:** Complete one recurring business loop and establish the habits that keep its context useful over time.

### Lesson 11 — Capstone: run one recurring business workflow

**Objective:** Execute an end-to-end workflow using approved persistent context, a reusable skill, and explicit review points.  
**Learner output:** One reviewed business or content artifact plus one confirmed learning.

Workflow:

1. Retrieve the active goal, project state, and relevant sources.
2. Confirm what is current and authoritative.
3. Run the selected reusable skill.
4. Inspect the plan, tools, permissions, and missing information.
5. Produce the artifact or decision preparation.
6. Verify facts, instructions, quality, privacy, and risk.
7. Request one diagnostic revision.
8. Approve or reject the result.
9. Propose memory candidates.
10. Confirm and store only the learning that should persist.

### Lesson 12 — Maintain, share, and improve the workspace

**Objective:** Create a sustainable review routine for accuracy, privacy, access, and workflow quality.  
**Learner output:** A weekly review checklist and a next-workflow brief.

Key points:

- Review active goals, unresolved questions, recent decisions, stale knowledge, and duplicate memories.
- Correct canonical sources and supersede outdated decisions without erasing useful history.
- Keep private, project, team, and organization knowledge explicitly separated.
- Audit connections and permissions before inviting collaborators.
- Measure whether the workflow saves time and improves decisions or quality.
- Expand autonomy only after narrow, repeated, reviewable success.

Final rule: **keep business truth inspectable, memory curated, skills testable, and consequential decisions human-owned.**

# Capstone rubric

| Criterion | Successful evidence |
| --------- | ------------------- |
| Architecture | Canonical sources, retrieval, skills, tools, and approvals have distinct roles. |
| Context quality | The workflow uses current, approved, relevant sources. |
| Memory judgment | Only confirmed, reusable information is preserved. |
| Provenance | Important knowledge includes source, scope, owner, status, and time. |
| Skill quality | Inputs, process, output, checks, and stop conditions are explicit. |
| Verification | Facts, instructions, quality, privacy, and risk are reviewed. |
| Maintenance | Correction, supersession, archiving, deletion, and backup are possible. |
| Business value | The recurring workflow addresses a real, reviewable business need. |
